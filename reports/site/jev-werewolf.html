Can Jev spot the wolf?

We asked TypeSafe's Jev model to judge who is lying in games of Werewolf and Mafia, then compared it with random guessing, simple text baselines, and a local LLM. We tested it on games written by LLMs, on games played by people, and in live games where Jev's pick decides the vote.

Every number on this page comes from the experiment's report files (reports/*.md). Brackets are 95% confidence intervals from a bootstrap that resamples whole games.

Setup

The game, the data, the score

The game

Werewolf with 9 players: 2 wolves, 1 Seer, 6 villagers. Wolves kill at night; everyone talks and votes by day. The judge reads the public transcript up to the current round and gives each player a probability of being a wolf.

The data

200 games we generated with llama3.1:8b playing every seat, plus four outside datasets: human Mafia (44 games), GPT-4o Mafia (35), Avalon (20) and spoken Werewolf (191). Then 100 stress games with gpt-oss:20b wolves and 300 live games.

The score

AUC: the chance a random wolf gets a higher score than a random villager. 0.5 is guessing, 1.0 is perfect. ECE: how far stated probabilities are from observed rates (lower is better). Each player in each round counts once.

      What changes Jev's accuracy

      Criteria wording

      Criteria that mention "deflecting blame" make Jev worse. In these games villagers, not wolves, say "deflect" (7.1% vs 1.7% of statements).

      Including the accused player's defense

      Leaving the defense out helps a little on both game sets. We didn't plan this comparison in advance, so it needs a confirming run.

      Limits and next steps

      • No judge beats chance on human games. Every positive result is on LLM-written text, and part of it is style (how a model writes) rather than lying.
      • Small samples. 50 live games per condition leaves wide intervals. On the generated games, whether jev_batched beats random and length is close (p ≈ 0.06–0.07). About 250 more games would settle it.
      • Mixed-model games leak. When wolves and villagers are different models, a length count alone gets AUC 0.61. A fair stronger-liar test needs every seat on the same model.
      • Not yet run: a frontier Claude judge (built and tested, needs an API key; estimated $38–114). Also confirming runs for the no-defense gain and for adaptive wolves evading Jev.
